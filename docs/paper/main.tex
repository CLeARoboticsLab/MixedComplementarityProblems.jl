\begin{abstract}
Mixed complementarity problems (MCPs) arise as the first-order optimality conditions of
nonlinear programs and noncooperative games, and give a natural formulation for the
multi-agent trajectory optimization problems that appear throughout robotics. The
dominant solver for problems of this form is \textsf{PATH}, a mature but closed-source
package. We present \textsf{MixedComplementarityProblems.jl}, an open-source, pure
Julia implementation of an interior-point method for parametric MCPs that (i) matches
\textsf{PATH}'s reliability on standard benchmarks and (ii) natively supports batched
solving, in which many parameter instances are solved simultaneously and in parallel,
either across CPU threads or on an NVIDIA GPU, behind a single solver implementation.
On a multi-agent lane-change trajectory game representative of robotics planning
problems, our CPU-multithreaded batched solver clears a batch of parametric instances
60--80$\times$ faster than sequential calls to \textsf{PATH}, and our GPU backend
clears the same batches roughly $20\times$ faster than \textsf{PATH}\todo{finalize the
GPU-vs-CPU comparison for the trajectory game once the latest benchmark numbers are
in---earlier runs showed GPU trailing CPU here, but that may no longer hold}. We
describe the solver's interior-point formulation, the abstraction that lets a single
solver implementation run unmodified across dense, batched-sparse, and single-large
linear-algebra backends, and report benchmarks against \textsf{PATH} on both randomly
generated quadratic programs and trajectory games.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multi-agent trajectory planning problems in robotics---lane changes and merges,
intersection negotiation, racing, human-robot interaction---are naturally posed as
noncooperative dynamic games, in which each agent optimizes its own trajectory subject
to the others' choices. The first-order (KKT) conditions of such a game, and of the
constrained trajectory optimization problems that underlie it, take the form of a
mixed complementarity problem (MCP). MCPs are therefore not a peripheral formalism for
this class of problems; they are the computational object that planners in this space
ultimately have to solve, often many times per planning cycle.

The dominant solver for problems of this form is \textsf{PATH}~\cite{dirkse1995path}, accessed
from Julia through the \texttt{PATHSolver.jl}/\texttt{ParametricMCPs.jl} stack. \textsf{PATH}
is mature and reliable, but it is closed-source, solves one parameter instance at a
time, and was not designed to sit inside a differentiable pipeline or underneath an
outer loop that itself needs to be parallelized. That is a real limitation for the
workloads robotics increasingly relies on: scenario- or sampling-based planning,
receding-horizon replanning across many candidate initial conditions, and learning
formulations that differentiate through a game's equilibrium. Each of these needs many
structurally identical MCPs solved together, not one instance solved in isolation.

No existing tool combines an open, modifiable solver with native support for solving
many such instances at once, in parallel, on the hardware a robotics practitioner
already has. Interior-point methods are a natural fit for closing this gap: they are
simple enough to reimplement and adapt to a new application, they are straightforward
to differentiate through, and their dominant per-iteration cost---a linear solve
against the KKT system---parallelizes cleanly across a batch of instances that share a
common sparsity structure, whether that parallelism is spread across CPU threads or
onto a GPU.

We present \textsf{MixedComplementarityProblems.jl}, an open-source, pure Julia
interior-point solver for parametric MCPs. Concretely, we contribute:
\begin{itemize}
  \item An interior-point method for parametric MCPs that matches \textsf{PATH}'s
  reliability on standard benchmarks, while remaining a compact, modifiable, pure
  Julia implementation that differentiates through its own solutions via
  \texttt{ChainRulesCore}/\texttt{ForwardDiff}.
  \item A native \emph{batched} solve mode, in which many parameter instances sharing
  one MCP structure are solved simultaneously, parallelized across CPU threads or an
  NVIDIA GPU behind a single solver implementation.
  \item An abstraction boundary---a small verb set (residual, Jacobian assembly,
  factorize, solve) between the interior-point loop and its linear-algebra
  backend---that lets that one implementation run unmodified across dense,
  batched-sparse, and single-large-instance regimes, so that supporting a new device or
  linear-algebra strategy never requires touching the solver loop itself.
\end{itemize}

As Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser} previews and Section~\ref{sec:experiments} quantifies, on a
multi-agent lane-change trajectory game representative of robotics planning problems,
clearing a batch of parametric instances with our batched solver takes a small fraction
of the wall-clock time required to clear the same batch with sequential calls to
\textsf{PATH}. The rest of this report is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:background} reviews MCPs and their connection to KKT conditions and
games. Section~\ref{sec:design} describes the interior-point method and its
strategy/device abstraction. Section~\ref{sec:batched} details the batched CPU and GPU
backends. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports benchmarks against \textsf{PATH} on
quadratic programs and trajectory games. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses
limitations and future work.
